# Resources: Tools, Books, Sites, and Communities for Jeopardy! Prep

This is an annotated ledger of every external resource referenced in the Jeopardy! Training System. It is written for an experienced trivia competitor who already has quiz league experience and wants to layer on Jeopardy!-specific preparation. Each entry describes what it is, who it's for, cost, time investment, and where it fits into the training system defined in `training-system.md` and the drill files in `drills/`. The "Minimum Viable Kit" at the end identifies the 4 items needed to start Week 1 today at zero cost.

---

## Online Tools and Websites

### J-Archive

- **What it is:** Fan-maintained database of over 560,000 past Jeopardy! clues spanning 40+ seasons. Searchable by category, season, date, and contestant. Includes full game boards, Daily Double locations, Final Jeopardy clues, and Coryat-ready scoring data.
- **Who it's for:** All levels. The single most important resource in the kit.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Time investment:** 20–30 min per game played; the training system assumes 2–4 games per week.
- **Where it fits:** Core tool for Coryat scoring (`drills/knowledge-drills.md`), full-game simulation (`drills/buzzer-drills.md`, Drill 4), and category-gap identification. Referenced in every drill file.
- **URL:** https://j-archive.com

### J! Scorer

- **What it is:** Companion scoring tool that sits alongside J-Archive games. Tracks Coryat scores, tags categories, and logs performance over time. Automates the manual Coryat tracking process.
- **Who it's for:** All levels. Especially useful once you're playing 2+ J-Archive games per week and want to spot trends.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Time investment:** Adds ~2 min of setup per game; saves ~5 min of manual scoring.
- **Where it fits:** Used in the Coryat scoring protocol (`drills/knowledge-drills.md`) and the baseline assessment (Weeks 1–2 of `training-system.md`).
- **URL:** https://j-scorer.com

### The Buzzer App

- **What it is:** Browser-based buzzer-timing trainer created by Fritz Holznagel. Plays audio clues, illuminates a simulated indicator light after a configurable delay (1000ms, 400ms, 130ms, or 0ms), and enforces a 250ms lockout for early clicks. Records reaction times across sessions for trend tracking.
- **Who it's for:** All levels. The primary tool for Buzzer Drills 1 and 2 in `drills/buzzer-drills.md`.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Time investment:** 10–15 min per drill session; the training system calls for 2–3 buzzer sessions per week.
- **Where it fits:** Core tool for `drills/buzzer-drills.md` Drills 1 (Light-Reaction) and 2 (Voice-Anticipation). Compatible with keyboard spacebar, mouse click, or Delcom USB button.
- **URL:** https://thebuzzerapp.com

### TheJeopardyFan

- **What it is:** Fan-operated site with daily game recaps, Coryat score tracking, Final Jeopardy wagering analysis, and contestant statistics. Publishes the "300 Club" (games with 30+ correct responses) and wagering strategy guides.
- **Who it's for:** Intermediate to advanced. Most useful once you're tracking your own Coryat scores and want benchmark comparisons.
- **Cost:** Free (basic); optional premium tier for deeper analysis.
- **Time investment:** 10–15 min/day for recaps; occasional deep-dives for wagering strategy.
- **Where it fits:** Wagering strategy reference (`drills/wagering-drills.md`); Coryat benchmarking (`drills/knowledge-drills.md`); cited in `prep-strategies-research.md` for champion statistics.
- **URL:** https://thejeopardyfan.com

### The Final Wager

- **What it is:** Educational blog and YouTube series by Keith Williams (2003 College Championship winner) teaching Final Jeopardy wagering using game-theory principles. Includes daily game breakdowns with optimal-wager analysis. The definitive resource for FJ wagering math.
- **Who it's for:** Intermediate to advanced. Essential for anyone past the audition stage.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Time investment:** 2–3 hours to learn the core framework (tutorial + first 10 breakdowns); 5–10 min/day to follow daily analysis.
- **Where it fits:** Core reference for `drills/wagering-drills.md` — the two-stage FJ calculation, game classification system, and Stratton's Dilemma all draw from Williams' framework. Cited throughout `prep-strategies-research.md`.
- **URL:** https://thefinalwager.wordpress.com

### JBoard.tv

- **What it is:** One of the oldest dedicated Jeopardy! fan forums. Features discussion boards, game analysis, strategy threads, and shared resources including Pavlov clue lists. More focused and serious than general fan communities.
- **Who it's for:** Intermediate to advanced. Good for finding curated Pavlov lists, discussing wagering edge cases, and connecting with former contestants.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Time investment:** Variable; primarily a reference and community resource.
- **Where it fits:** Source for Pavlov clue lists (`drills/knowledge-drills.md`); community resource for strategy discussion. Colin Davy's category-frequency analysis, cited in `drills/knowledge-drills.md`, originated in JBoard discussions.
- **URL:** https://www.jboard.tv

### Jeopardy.com Prep Center

- **What it is:** Official resource from Sony/Jeopardy! Productions. Hosts the Jeopardy! Anytime Test (the primary audition pathway), practice tests, J!6 daily clues written by the show's writers, and contestant preparation articles including "6 Test Tips From The Jeopardy! Team."
- **Who it's for:** All levels. The mandatory first stop for anyone entering the audition process.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Time investment:** 30 min for the Anytime Test; 10 min/day for J!6 clues.
- **Where it fits:** Referenced in `prep-strategies-research.md` (Audition/Contestant-Search Process section) and `training-system.md` (Pre-Audition Week protocol).
- **URL:** https://www.jeopardy.com/be-on-j/prep-center

### BuzzerBlog — "So, You Got The Call"

- **What it is:** Blog collecting first-person accounts and advice from former Jeopardy! contestants. The "So, You Got The Call" section is specifically for people who have been invited to tape, covering logistics, stage prep, buzzer strategy, and what to expect on tape day.
- **Who it's for:** Advanced (post-audition). Essential reading after receiving the call.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Time investment:** 1–2 hours to read the core articles; revisit before taping.
- **Where it fits:** Cited in `drills/buzzer-drills.md` for buzzer strategy, `training-system.md` for pre-taping protocol, and `prep-strategies-research.md` for stage-performance advice.
- **URL:** https://www.buzzerblog.com/thecall/

### Jeopardad.com

- **What it is:** Personal blog by a Jeopardy! contestant documenting his preparation process, including detailed buzzer strategy analysis (voice-based vs. light-based timing methods) and practice protocols.
- **Who it's for:** Intermediate. Useful for buzzer strategy nuance.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Time investment:** 1 hour to read the key articles.
- **Where it fits:** Cited in `drills/buzzer-drills.md` for the voice-anticipation timing method.
- **URL:** https://www.jeopardad.com

## Books

### *Secrets of the Buzzer* — Fritz Holznagel (self-published, 2019; 2nd ed. 2021)

- **What it is:** A focused 57-page deep dive into buzzer mechanics, reaction times, physiology, and strategy by the 1995 Tournament of Champions winner. Based on 100+ hours of systematic experimentation. The 2nd edition includes a foreword by James Holzhauer.
- **Who it's for:** Intermediate to advanced. Essential for anyone called to tape; highly recommended for anyone in active training.
- **Cost:** ~$5–10 (ebook/paperback).
- **Free alternative:** The Buzzer App (same author) covers the core concepts in practice form. The book adds the research and rationale.
- **Time investment:** 2–3 hours to read; ongoing practice to implement.
- **Where it fits:** The theoretical foundation for all four drills in `drills/buzzer-drills.md`. Reaction-time benchmarks (228ms → 126ms) and grip/posture optimization are drawn directly from this book.

### *Brainiac* — Ken Jennings (Villard, 2006)

- **What it is:** Jennings' memoir covering his record 74-game winning streak, interwoven with a cultural history of trivia. More inspirational/contextual than practical — gives insight into the mindset and preparation habits of the most successful Jeopardy! contestant in history.
- **Who it's for:** All levels. Good for motivation and understanding the competitive culture.
- **Cost:** ~$10–16 (paperback); widely available at public libraries.
- **Free alternative:** None needed — this is a supplementary read, not a study tool.
- **Time investment:** 6–10 hours to read.
- **Where it fits:** Suggested passive-intake reading on rest days in `training-system.md`. Cited in `prep-strategies-research.md` for Jennings' views on knowledge breadth vs. depth.

### *Prisoner of Trebekistan* — Bob Harris (Crown, 2006)

- **What it is:** Harris's memoir covering 10 years of Jeopardy! tournament appearances. More practically useful than *Brainiac* — includes specific study methods, learning strategies, and detailed accounts of how he prepared for Tournament of Champions appearances.
- **Who it's for:** Intermediate. Valuable for study-method ideas and understanding the multi-appearance contestant mindset.
- **Cost:** ~$10–16 (paperback); available at libraries.
- **Free alternative:** None needed — supplementary reading.
- **Time investment:** 6–10 hours to read.
- **Where it fits:** Cited in `prep-strategies-research.md` for Harris's study protocols. Useful background for the category deep-dive approach in `drills/knowledge-drills.md`.

### *Mythology* — Edith Hamilton (Little, Brown, 1942; many reprints)

- **What it is:** The standard reference for Greek and Roman mythology. Multiple former contestants report that this single book covers ~90% of mythology clues on the show.
- **Who it's for:** All levels, especially those with a mythology gap.
- **Cost:** ~$8–12 (paperback); widely available at libraries.
- **Free alternative:** Wikipedia's mythology articles cover the same material but with less narrative structure.
- **Time investment:** 8–12 hours to read.
- **Where it fits:** Category-specific reference for the gap-closing protocol in `drills/knowledge-drills.md`. Use when mythology appears in the bottom-3 categories during Coryat tracking. (Opinion: one of the highest-ROI category-specific books because mythology clues recur frequently and draw from a relatively small canon.)

### *Word Power Made Easy* — Norman Lewis (Pocket Books, 1949; many reprints)

- **What it is:** Classic vocabulary and etymology builder. Helps with word-origin clues, "Before & After" categories, and the etymological reasoning that underlies many Jeopardy! clue constructions.
- **Who it's for:** Beginners to intermediate, especially those who want to improve on wordplay and vocabulary categories.
- **Cost:** ~$8–12 (paperback); available at libraries.
- **Free alternative:** Free etymology resources online (Etymonline.com).
- **Time investment:** Several weeks at 20–30 min/day.
- **Where it fits:** Supplementary resource for vocabulary-related category gaps identified in `drills/knowledge-drills.md`.

## Hardware

### Delcom USB Handheld Button (~$63)

- **What it is:** A USB-connected handheld button that mimics the shape and resistance profile of the Jeopardy! signaling device more closely than any consumer alternative. Plugs into a computer and registers as a USB input device, compatible with The Buzzer App and any keyboard-input-based practice tool.
- **Who it's for:** Intermediate to advanced. Recommended for anyone who has been called to tape or is in serious active training (Phase 2+).
- **Cost:** ~$63 (Delcom Products direct or Amazon). This is the only paid hardware in the kit.
- **Free alternative:** A clicky retractable pen. Multiple champions (including James Holzhauer) practiced with a pen before upgrading. The pen is sufficient through Phase 1 and into Phase 2.
- **Time investment:** Used during all buzzer drill sessions (10–35 min each, 2–3 times per week).
- **Where it fits:** Physical buzzer device for `drills/buzzer-drills.md` Drills 1–4. Pairs with The Buzzer App for Drills 1 and 2.
- **URL:** https://www.delcomproducts.com

## Spaced Repetition and Flashcards

### Anki

- **What it is:** Open-source spaced repetition flashcard application. Uses an algorithm to schedule reviews at optimal intervals for long-term retention. The most widely recommended study tool among former Jeopardy! champions for building and maintaining broad knowledge.
- **Who it's for:** All levels. The single most recommended study tool by former contestants for knowledge breadth.
- **Cost:** Free (desktop, AnkiDroid for Android). AnkiMobile for iOS is $24.99.
- **Time investment:** 20–45 min/day for meaningful progress; consistency matters more than session length.
- **Where it fits:** Used in the gap-closing protocol in `drills/knowledge-drills.md` for building custom decks around weak categories and Pavlov clues. Referenced in `training-system.md` Phase 2 (Build) for spaced repetition sessions.
- **URL:** https://apps.ankiweb.net

### Pavlov Clue Lists (Community-Created)

- **What it is:** Curated lists of recurring clue-answer pairs that appear on Jeopardy! with disproportionate frequency. Named for the idea that hearing the key phrase should trigger an automatic response. Examples: "Finnish composer" → Sibelius; "Canterbury Tales author" → Chaucer. Lists of 500–2,000 pairs are shared on JBoard.tv and Reddit r/Jeopardy.
- **Who it's for:** All levels. Foundational preparation material.
- **Cost:** Free (community-shared lists on JBoard.tv).
- **Time investment:** 5–10 hours to compile a personal list from J-Archive data; or download a curated list and load into Anki. Then 15 min/session, 2 sessions/week as specified in `training-system.md`.
- **Where it fits:** Core study material for `drills/knowledge-drills.md` (Pavlov clue study protocol). The training system calls for 2 Pavlov sessions per week during Phase 2. Designed so that ~60% of Pavlov knowledge transfers to general trivia leagues, while ~40% is Jeopardy!-writer-specific (see transfer classification in `training-system.md`).

- **Who it's for:** All levels. Good for staying current with show trends, finding Pavlov lists, and connecting with other aspiring contestants.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Time investment:** Variable; browse as needed. Not a training tool, but a useful reference.
- **Where it fits:** Source for Daily Double heatmap data cited in `prep-strategies-research.md`; community resource for finding Anki decks and Pavlov lists.

- **What it is:** See entry under "Online Tools" above. Also functions as the primary serious-contestant community.
- **Where it fits:** Community + resource. Pavlov lists, strategy discussion, former-contestant advice.

## Geography-Specific Tools

### Seterra

- **What it is:** Free geography quiz platform with map-based quizzes covering countries, capitals, rivers, mountains, and flags. Available as a website and mobile app.
- **Who it's for:** All levels. Geography is one of the most common Jeopardy! categories.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Time investment:** 10–15 min/day when geography is an identified weak category.
- **Where it fits:** Supplementary tool for the gap-closing protocol in `drills/knowledge-drills.md` when geography appears in the bottom-3 categories.
- **URL:** https://www.seterra.com

### Sporcle

- **What it is:** Trivia quiz platform with thousands of category-based quizzes (geography, history, sports, pop culture, science). Timed format adds a speed-recall component.
- **Who it's for:** All levels. Good for quick targeted practice on specific topics.
- **Cost:** Free (with ads); optional premium tier.
- **Time investment:** 10–20 min/session.
- **Where it fits:** Supplementary tool for category gap-closing in `drills/knowledge-drills.md`. Useful for rapid-fire recall practice in specific weak areas.
- **URL:** https://www.sporcle.com

## Minimum Viable Kit (Start Today, $0)

These 4 items are everything needed to run Week 1 of the training system as written in `training-system.md`:

1. **J-Archive** (https://j-archive.com) — Play 3–4 games with Coryat scoring to establish your knowledge baseline. Tag categories to identify your top-3 and bottom-3. This is the first task in the Week 1–2 Baseline phase.

2. **The Buzzer App** (https://thebuzzerapp.com) — Run Buzzer Drill 1 (Light-Reaction) at 1000ms delay to establish your reaction-time baseline. Three sessions of 50 buzzes each. Record your median RT in `progress-tracker.md`.

3. **A clicky retractable pen** — Your buzzer stand-in for Drill 3 (Rapid-Click Endurance) and Drill 4 (Full-Game Simulation). Multiple champions practiced this way. Upgrade to the Delcom USB button ($63) when you enter Phase 2.

4. **`progress-tracker.md`** — The tracking template included in this kit. Print it or use it digitally to record Coryat scores, buzzer RT, weak categories, wagering decisions, and trivia league results from day one.

**Total cost: $0.** All four are free and available immediately. The training system is designed so that no paid resource is required until Phase 2 (Weeks 3–6), when *Secrets of the Buzzer* (~$5–10) and optionally the Delcom button (~$63) become recommended.
