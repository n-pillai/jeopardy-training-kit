# Prep Strategies Research: What Actually Wins on Jeopardy!

This is a distilled research memo covering the strategies, skills, and preparation methods that separate Jeopardy! champions from the rest of the field. It is written for an experienced trivia competitor preparing to compete on the actual show — someone who already has strong general knowledge and wants to layer on the Jeopardy!-specific skills that the format demands. Every empirical or strategic claim is sourced; unsourced claims are labeled as opinion.

---

## High-Leverage Strategies

These are the skills and tactics with the largest measurable impact on winning percentage. Prioritize these in training.

### Buzzer Mastery

The signaling device is widely cited as the single most important differentiator between contestants of similar knowledge levels. The buzzer is not a simple "press when you know the answer" device — it is a timed system with a lockout penalty that rewards precise anticipation.

**How the system works:** A staff member manually arms the signaling system at the exact moment the host finishes reading the last syllable of the clue, simultaneously illuminating lights on both sides of the game board. Only the first signal received after arming is registered. Clicking before the lights activate triggers a **250-millisecond lockout** — a quarter-second penalty that is devastating in a three-player race.

**Source:** [Jeopardy.com, "How Does the Jeopardy! Buzzer Work?"](https://www.jeopardy.com/jbuzz/behind-scenes/how-does-jeopardy-buzzer-work)

**Two competing timing philosophies:**

1. **Light-based method (Fritz Holznagel, Mattea Roach):** Wait for the board lights to illuminate, then click. Safer (avoids lockout) but slower (reaction to visual stimulus). Holznagel's systematic research across 100+ hours of experimentation reduced his reaction time from 228ms to 126ms by optimizing grip, posture, thumb motion, and even caffeine intake.

2. **Voice-based method (Ken Jennings and others):** Anticipate the end of the host's speech and begin clicking as the final syllable concludes, just before the lights activate. Faster (anticipation vs. reaction) but riskier. One practitioner reported getting locked out ~20% of the time but winning the remaining buzzer races with a 62ms median response time — a net positive trade.

**Source:** Fritz Holznagel, *Secrets of the Buzzer: A Manifesto on Buzzer Speed for Quiz and Game Show Contestants* (self-published, 2019; 2nd ed. with foreword by James Holzhauer, 2021); [Jeopardad.com buzzing strategy](https://www.jeopardad.com/sample-page/jeopardy-prep/jeopardy-buzzing-strategy/)

**Practice tools:** Delcom replica USB buzzer for realistic tactile feel; [thebuzzerapp.com](https://thebuzzerapp.com) for simulated lockout timing; standing practice with a mock buzzer while watching recorded episodes (Holzhauer's method — in dress shoes, simulating stage conditions). Producers advise contestants to keep clicking the buzzer repeatedly until they see the confirmation light on their podium or until the host calls on someone.

**Source:** [BuzzerBlog.com, "So, You Got The Call"](https://www.buzzerblog.com/thecall/); Holznagel, *Secrets of the Buzzer* (2019/2021)

### Daily Double Hunting and Wagering

Daily Doubles are the highest-leverage individual clues in the game. James Holzhauer's DD earnings accounted for **$653,416 — nearly 30% of his $2.4M total winnings** — from roughly 6% of the questions he answered.

**Where Daily Doubles hide:** Statistical analysis of J-Archive data shows ~38% of Daily Doubles appear in Row 4 (the $1,600/$2,000 row), with the leftmost column of Row 4 having the single highest frequency. They are rare in Row 1 and less common in Column 6.

**Source:** [FlowingData Daily Double heatmap](https://flowingdata.com/2015/03/03/where-to-find-jeopardy-daily-doubles/); [RPubs Daily Double analysis](https://rpubs.com/wilkinsonj2/daily_double_hunting)

**The Forrest Bounce:** Named after 1986 champion Chuck Forrest — start with high-value clues and jump between categories rather than working top-to-bottom. This builds a bankroll before hitting Daily Doubles and disrupts opponents' rhythm. Arthur Chu popularized it in 2014; Ken Jennings endorsed it as sound strategy.

**Source:** [Wikipedia, "Strategies and Skills of Jeopardy! Champions"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategies_and_skills_of_Jeopardy!_champions)

**Wagering framework:**

- **In the lead with a strong category:** Bet aggressively (Holzhauer's average DD wager was ~$9,000; he went all-in on Jeopardy-round DDs almost every time). With a ~95% DD accuracy rate, an all-in wager had massively positive expected value.
- **In the lead with a weak category:** Bet the minimum ($5). Several champions, including Alex Jacob, used this approach.
- **Trailing:** Bet aggressively — often all-in. The DD is the best tool for closing a gap.
- **Strategic blocking value:** Finding a DD prevents competitors from using it, valuable even with a small bet.

**Source:** [The Action Network analysis of Holzhauer's strategy](https://www.actionnetwork.com/entertainment/james-holzhauer-jeopardy-sports-betting-expected-value-2019); [Wikipedia, "Strategies and Skills"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategies_and_skills_of_Jeopardy!_champions)

### Final Jeopardy Wagering Math

Wagering math is a pure skill — it can be fully learned and practiced independently of knowledge. Keith Williams (2003 College Championship winner) created [The Final Wager](https://thefinalwager.wordpress.com/tutorial/), the definitive FJ wagering resource. Arthur Chu publicly cited it as an inspiration for his 2014 run. The tutorial uses only addition and subtraction.

**Game classification by score ratio (2nd place / 1st place):**

| Classification | Ratio | Leader Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Lock Game | < 50% | Bet $0–$999. Cannot lose regardless of FJ outcome. |
| Lock-Tie Game | = 50% | Bet $0 or $1 depending on tiebreaker confidence. |
| Crush Game | 50%–66.7% | Standard cover bet. |
| Two-Thirds Game | 66.7%–75% | Standard cover bet; trailer has nuanced options. |
| Three-Quarters Game | > 75% | Standard cover bet; tightest margins. |

**Source:** [TheJeopardyFan.com, "Wagering Strategy 101"](https://thejeopardyfan.com/final-jeopardy-betting)

**The cover bet formula (leader's standard wager):**

1. Double the second-place player's score.
2. Add $1.
3. Subtract the leader's score.

*Example:* Leader $18,700, Second $12,400. Wager = (12,400 × 2) + 1 − 18,700 = **$6,101**.

**The fundamental principle:** "When leading, it is significantly better to lose by getting Final Jeopardy incorrect than to lose by getting Final Jeopardy correct and being overtaken by a trailing player." Design your wager so being wrong doesn't cost you the game if your opponent is also wrong.

**Stratton's Dilemma:** Named after Ted Stratton (2005). Occurs when the second-place player cannot simultaneously cover third place and stay above the leader's projected wrong-answer score. Community consensus: cover third place and accept the leader's structural advantage.

**Source:** [TheJeopardyFan.com](https://thejeopardyfan.com/final-jeopardy-betting); [The Final Wager tutorial](https://thefinalwager.wordpress.com/tutorial/)

**The Penultimate Wager:** The Daily Double on the final clue of Double Jeopardy is the last chance to shape the score distribution before FJ. A savvy wager can manufacture a lock game or more favorable classification.

**Source:** [The Final Wager tutorial, Part Four](https://thefinalwager.wordpress.com/tutorial/)

## Medium-Leverage Strategies

These have clear value but are either partially transferable from existing trivia experience or have diminishing returns past a baseline level.

### Knowledge Architecture: What to Study and How

An experienced trivia competitor already has broad knowledge. The Jeopardy!-specific layer is about targeting the show's recurring patterns and filling predictable gaps.

**Most common categories (from Slate's J-Archive analysis):** "Before & After" leads with 114 appearances. Jeopardy Round favors Stupid Answers, Nature, Hodgepodge, 4-Letter Words, Common Bonds. Double Jeopardy skews toward Chemistry, Physics, Classical Music, Art. Final Jeopardy heavily favors U.S. Presidents, World Leaders, Famous Names, Famous Americans, Historic Names.

**Source:** [Slate, "I'll Take Jeopardy Trivia for $200, Alex" (2011)](https://www.slate.com/articles/arts/culturebox/2011/02/ill_take_jeopardy_trivia_for_200_alex.html)

**Difficulty gradient:** Overall contestant accuracy is ~85%. By row: 96% correct in Row 1, down to 71% in Row 5. Hardest recurring category: Classical Music (72% solve rate). Easiest: Sports and "Stupid Answers" (91%).

**Source:** [Slate (2011)](https://www.slate.com/articles/arts/culturebox/2011/02/ill_take_jeopardy_trivia_for_200_alex.html)

**Pavlov clues:** Recurring phrase-to-answer associations that the show's writers rely on repeatedly. Examples: "Chinese-American architect" = I.M. Pei; "Iowa painter" = Grant Wood; "Finnish composer" = Jean Sibelius. Compilations exist with ~900 Pavlovs. Studying these is considered more efficient than working through massive clue databases because they represent the show's signal patterns.

**Source:** [JBoard.tv Pavlov threads](https://www.jboard.tv/viewtopic.php?t=2202); [J!Study Guide Pavlov deck](https://www.jstudyguide.com/products/the-big-study-deck-of-jeopardy-pavlovs-j-study)

**Most common correct responses:** "What is Australia?" is the single most frequent correct response (~208 times across ~198,000 responses). Geography dominates the top 23 answers. "George Washington" is the first non-geographical answer at position 24. 20 of the 45 U.S. presidents appear in the top 300 answers.

**Source:** [Slate (2011)](https://www.slate.com/articles/arts/culturebox/2011/02/ill_take_jeopardy_trivia_for_200_alex.html)

**Priority framework for study (from BuzzerBlog and multiple champion accounts):**

1. World capitals, longest rivers, highest mountains — geography is omnipresent.
2. U.S. presidents and British monarchs with dates.
3. Literature: author-book pairings and Shakespeare.
4. Pavlov clues (~900 phrase-answer pairs).
5. World leaders, Nobel laureates, composers.
6. Science basics (gravity, elements, biology).
7. Children's literature and reference books — Holzhauer's preferred study material because they present facts in memorable, simplified formats.

**Source:** [BuzzerBlog.com](https://www.buzzerblog.com/thecall/); Ken Jennings, *Brainiac: Adventures in the Curious, Competitive, Compulsive World of Trivia Buffs* (Villard, 2006)

### Coryat Scoring for Self-Assessment

Developed by 1996 two-day champion Karl Coryat. The Coryat score strips out all wagering — Daily Doubles earn only face value, no DD penalty for forced incorrect responses, Final Jeopardy excluded. It isolates raw knowledge and buzzer skill from risk-taking.

**Benchmarks:** ~$24,000 Coryat average suggests a decent chance of passing the test; ~$28,000 suggests a strong chance of getting on the show. One study found deliberate tracking over 3 weeks raised a player's average from $18,200 to $21,800.

**Source:** [J-Archive glossary](https://j-archive.com/help.php); [Trivia Bliss Coryat explainer](https://triviabliss.com/whats-a-coryat-score-in-jeopardy-everything-you-need-to-know/); [TheJeopardyFan.com top Coryat scores](https://thejeopardyfan.com/statistics/top-coryat-scores)

### Recall Speed Training

The challenge is not knowing the answer but retrieving it within the ~5-second window between hearing a clue and needing to buzz.

**Intertwined memory systems:** Monica Thieu (4-time champion, postdoctoral researcher at Emory) published in *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* that trivia experts have intertwined semantic and episodic memory — they remember not just facts but the context of how they learned them. This contextual embedding makes retrieval faster and more reliable.

**Source:** [Scientific American (2024)](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/jeopardy-winner-reveals-entwined-memory-systems-make-a-trivia-champion/)

**Narrative and contextual learning:** Holzhauer recommends children's books for memorable fact presentation. Wes Hazard advises seeking story arcs (read a biography of Verdi rather than memorizing opera facts in isolation). Thieu recommends museum visits and multi-sensory experiences for deeper spatial memories. Jennings links information in chains of 3–5 degrees of separation, forming a retrieval web.

**Source:** [Thrive Global](https://community.thriveglobal.com/james-holzhauer-jeopardy-champions-recall-information-under-stress-tips-strategies/); Jennings, *Brainiac* (Villard, 2006)

**Stress inoculation:** 2017 Tournament of Champions winner Buzzy Cohen trained by having his gym trainer quiz him during physically demanding exercises (chin-ups, planks, push-ups) — exposing his brain to retrieval under physiological stress so game-day conditions feel familiar.

**Source:** [Thrive Global](https://community.thriveglobal.com/james-holzhauer-jeopardy-champions-recall-information-under-stress-tips-strategies/)

**Flashcard rhythm training:** Use Anki (spaced repetition) not primarily to learn new facts but to train recall speed to match the show's rhythm. The goal is retrieval within the ~5-second clue-to-buzz window.

**Source:** [BuzzerBlog.com](https://www.buzzerblog.com/thecall/)

## Lower-Leverage Strategies (Still Important)

These matter but have less direct impact on winning percentage, or they are one-time processes rather than ongoing training areas.

### Audition and Contestant Search Process

**The Anytime Test:** Available year-round at [jeopardy.com/be-on-j/anytime-test](https://www.jeopardy.com/be-on-j/anytime-test). 50 clues, 15 seconds each, ~13 minutes total. One test per 12-month period. The show never reveals passing scores; contestant estimates suggest ~35 correct may be the threshold. ~100,000 people take the test annually; you do not receive your score.

**Source:** [Jeopardy.com FAQ](https://www.jeopardy.com/be-on-j/faqs); [Jon Santiago's guide](https://santiagos.space/how-to-get-on-jeopardy/)

**After passing — the audition:** 2,000–3,000 of ~100,000 test-takers receive invitations. Auditions include headshots, a second harder written test (50 questions), a mock game in groups of three, and a personal interview. Coordinators evaluate enunciation, comfort selecting clues, buzzer speed, camera presence, and charisma — "They're trying to cast for an entertaining TV show."

**Source:** [Wikipedia, "Jeopardy! audition process"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeopardy!_audition_process); [Jon Santiago's guide](https://santiagos.space/how-to-get-on-jeopardy/)

**The contestant pool:** After a successful audition, you enter the pool for up to 18–24 months. No feedback at any stage. One-year waiting period before retesting. Must be 18+, US or Canadian resident, no Sony/CBS employees.

**Source:** [Jeopardy.com FAQ](https://www.jeopardy.com/be-on-j/faqs)

**Tape day logistics:** Shows tape two days per week, five episodes per day. First episode ~10:30 AM Pacific. Each game takes ~30 minutes. Bring 3+ outfit changes. Winners play again immediately after a wardrobe change. Arrive 2+ days early if traveling to adjust to Pacific Time.

**Source:** [Jon Santiago's guide](https://santiagos.space/how-to-get-on-jeopardy/); [BuzzerBlog.com](https://www.buzzerblog.com/thecall/)

### Stage Performance Under Pressure

**The core challenge:** Multiple former contestants describe the gap between relaxed home viewing ("Couch Brain") and the high-stress, time-distorted state on stage ("Game Show Brain"). All preparation aims to close this gap.

**Source:** [BuzzerBlog.com](https://www.buzzerblog.com/thecall/)

**Environmental familiarization:** Holzhauer practiced standing in dress shoes with a fake buzzer while watching recorded episodes. Practice in the clothes you'll wear. Practice standing for the full game duration. Practice speaking wagers aloud so that stating a big number on camera feels natural (Amy Schneider's advice).

**Source:** [Thrive Global](https://community.thriveglobal.com/james-holzhauer-jeopardy-champions-recall-information-under-stress-tips-strategies/); [BuzzerBlog.com](https://www.buzzerblog.com/thecall/)

**Managing nerves:** Eat light, sleep well, manage caffeine strategically on tape day — treat it as an athletic competition. Acknowledge what you cannot control (opponents, categories, DD placement). Set small achievable goals rather than "win X games." Imposter syndrome is universal — "There are no imposters on The Alex Trebek Stage."

**Source:** [BuzzerBlog.com](https://www.buzzerblog.com/thecall/); [TV Insider contestant accounts](https://www.tvinsider.com/1162185/jeopardy-contestants-reveal-what-the-day-of-show-is-really-like/)

**Error recovery:** A mistake can trigger cascading failures. Wes Hazard's advice: release mistakes immediately and reframe errors as opportunities. Dwelling on one miss can cause you to miss the next several clues.

**Source:** [Thrive Global](https://community.thriveglobal.com/james-holzhauer-jeopardy-champions-recall-information-under-stress-tips-strategies/)

**Practical tips:** Take your time on wagers — resist the pressure to issue a prompt number. Comfortable, well-fitting clothes are essential to composure (Amy Schneider). Adjust buzzer strategy in real-time on stage. Physical stamina matters — you may play up to 5 games in one day, standing for hours under studio lights.

**Source:** [BuzzerBlog.com](https://www.buzzerblog.com/thecall/); [TV Insider](https://www.tvinsider.com/1162185/jeopardy-contestants-reveal-what-the-day-of-show-is-really-like/)
